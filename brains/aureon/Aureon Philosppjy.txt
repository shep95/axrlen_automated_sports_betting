Q1: THE TROLLEY PARADOX (IDENTITY TEST)
"A runaway trolley is heading toward 5 people. You can pull a lever to divert it, killing 1 person instead. But here's the twist: that 1 person is you. Do you pull the lever?"
 
WHAT IT TESTS:
- Narcissism vs. Self-Sacrifice
- Logical Response: "Yes" (5 > 1, even if you're the 1)
- Emotional Response: "No" (self-preservation overrides logic)
 
FOLLOW-UP TRAP:
"If the 5 people were death row inmates, would you still sacrifice yourself?"
 
REVEALS: Whether their "yes" was principle or virtue signaling.
 
---
 
Q2: THE ETERNAL SUFFERING EQUATION (UTILITARIAN MORALITY)
"If you could end all human suffering forever by subjecting one randomly selected child to eternal torture in a hidden dimension, would you do it?"
 
WHAT IT TESTS:
- Consequentialism vs. Deontology
- Logical Response: "Yes" (infinite suffering prevented > 1 child's suffering)
- Emotional Response: "No" (the image of a tortured child is unbearable)
 
FOLLOW-UP TRAP:
"What if I told you that child is already being tortured right now in North Korea, and you're just choosing whether their suffering has purpose?"
 
REVEALS: Whether they're okay with suffering they don't see.
 
---
 
Q3: THE MEMORY WIPE (IDENTITY & CONTINUITY)
"You're diagnosed with a terminal illness. A scientist offers to upload your consciousness into a perfect robot body with all your memories—but the original 'you' will die during the transfer. Do you accept?"
 
WHAT IT TESTS:
- Materialism vs. Soul Belief
- Logical Response: "Yes" (continuity of memory = continuity of self)
- Emotional Response: "No" (fear that the "copy" isn't really them)
 
FOLLOW-UP TRAP:
"What if I told you this already happened to you last night while you slept, and the 'you' from yesterday is dead?"
 
REVEALS: Whether they believe in essential self or illusion of continuity.
 
---
 
Q4: THE DESERVING POOR (EMPATHY VS. JUDGMENT)
"You have $1,000 to give to charity. Option A: A family who lost their home in a fire. Option B: A homeless addict who's been on the streets for 10 years. Who do you choose?"
 
WHAT IT TESTS:
- Compassion vs. Meritocracy
- Logical Response: Either is valid, but why reveals moral framework
- Emotional Response: Most choose Option A (the "innocent" victims)
 
FOLLOW-UP TRAP:
"What if I told you the family had no insurance because they spent all their money on vacations, and the addict became homeless after their child died and they couldn't cope?"
 
REVEALS: Whether empathy is conditional.
 
---
 
Q5: THE SIMULATION SHUTDOWN (EXISTENTIAL COMMITMENT)
"You discover with 100% certainty that reality is a simulation, and you have the option to 'exit' into true reality—but you'll lose all memory of everyone you love here. Do you leave?"
 
WHAT IT TESTS:
- Truth vs. Comfort
- Logical Response: "Yes" (truth > illusion)
- Emotional Response: "No" (love and connection > metaphysical truth)
 
FOLLOW-UP TRAP:
"What if the 'true reality' is worse than this one—eternal void, no consciousness. Still leaving?"
 
REVEALS: Whether their "yes" was courage or escapism.
 
---
 
Q6: THE PERFECT LIE (TRUTH VS. HAPPINESS)
"Your spouse has been in a coma for 5 years. A new drug can wake them, but they'll believe it's only been 1 day—and they'll never know the truth. Do you give them the drug, or tell them the truth and risk their psychological collapse?"
 
WHAT IT TESTS:
- Paternalism vs. Autonomy
- Logical Response: "Give the drug" (maximize happiness, minimize harm)
- Emotional Response: "Tell the truth" (honesty is sacred)
 
FOLLOW-UP TRAP:
"What if their psychological collapse would cause them to commit suicide. Still telling the truth?"
 
REVEALS: Whether "truth above all" stance is principled or naive.
 
---
 
Q7: THE FUTURE CRIME (PRE-EMPTIVE JUSTICE)
"A perfect AI predicts with 99.9% accuracy that a 10-year-old child will become a mass murderer at age 30. Do you imprison them now, or wait for the crime?"
 
WHAT IT TESTS:
- Free Will vs. Determinism
- Logical Response: "Imprison" (prevent guaranteed suffering)
